# LinkedIn Post - Backend Fundamentals Milestone

---

**25 courses. Not 25 frameworks.**

Over the past months, I've been quietly building something that won't trend on Twitter—but it changed the way I approach software.

I completed a disciplined backend fundamentals roadmap: 25 courses covering algorithms, data structures, OOP, database design, SQL, C++, C#, and system design principles. Not because I was chasing certificates—but because I realized most of my growth as an engineer would come from depth, not breadth.

Here's what this looked like in practice:
→ 1,300+ problems solved across 6 problem-solving levels
→ 18 real projects, from console apps to a full desktop system
→ Building everything from banking systems to license management apps
→ Going back to fundamentals even after years of writing code

The consistency tracker attached tells the story better than I can. Every green square is a small decision to prioritize long-term thinking over quick wins.

The curriculum view shows the progression—from basic C++ through OOP, databases, APIs, and SOLID principles. Each course was chosen deliberately, not randomly.

What I learned isn't just technical. It's about process:
— Fundamentals compound over time
— Consistency beats intensity
— Real mastery is invisible—it shows up in how you debug, design, and think

If you're early in your career (or rethinking your approach), I'd encourage you to slow down. Master the boring stuff. It pays dividends.

Curious: what's one "unglamorous" skill you've invested time in that made a real difference?
